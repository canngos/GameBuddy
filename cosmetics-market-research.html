What coins can buy

The Season Pass is gone — there was no level or XP system for it to progress along. This is the survey of what could fill the shelf instead, measured against what Discord, Steam and the other places our players already spend actually sell.

31 August 2026 Companion to monetization-analysis.md Shipped alongside: 8 frames, 8 banners

The short version

Eleven candidates, ranked by what they return for what they cost. Everything here is decoration only — nothing touches matching, ranking or limits, which is what keeps us on the right side of the pay-to-win line the product has held so far.

The dating apps sell almost no cosmetics at all. Their entire catalogue is visibility. We should not read our monetization off them despite the shared swipe mechanic — the right comparables are Discord and Steam, which sell profile decoration to exactly the people who use GameBuddy.

Every one of those catalogues is broader in kind, not just in count. Discord sells four categories; Steam sells eight. We sell two. A ninth frame is worth less than the first item of a new kind, because a gamer who doesn't like rings currently has nothing else to spend on.

Does our pricing hold up?

A 500-coin pack is $1.99, so a coin is worth about $0.004. Converting the shelf back into money puts it beside what the market already charges:

The four worth building next

1

Animated banners

The whole pipeline exists: ProfileBanner already renders through expo-image with autoplay, and cosmetic.animated is an existing column. Cost is a generator function and a migration. Watch file size — a 1200×400 canvas is 5.5× the pixels of a frame, so keep loops short. Price at 900–1,400.

2

Limited-time drops

What the Season Pass was really for, without needing levels. Add available_from / available_until, filter in store(), and leave ownership alone so anything bought stays bought. Unlike a season it commits us to nothing: a drop that doesn't happen is invisible, where a season that doesn't happen is a broken promise on a card.

3

Username colour

A new NAME kind, an equipped slot, and the colour threaded through the eight places a display name is drawn. No art at all — the item is a token, not an image. Colour must come from theme role names rather than free hex, or the contrast gate in npm run check can't judge it.

4

Bundles

A matching frame and banner for less than the sum. Cleanest as a bundle row granting several ownership rows in one transaction — gamer_cosmetic.paid already records what was actually charged per item, so it survives repricing. Raises average spend without a single new drawing.

Not recommended

  • Loot boxes or gacha crates. Declined previously and the reasoning holds: no resale market, a two-second flex surface, loses us Belgium and the Netherlands, and pushes us into a simulated-gambling content rating. Revisit at 60–100+ cosmetics.
  • A second premium currency. We sell only cosmetics, so there is no pay-to-win line to protect — a second currency would just create unspendable remainders.
  • Stock avatars. Once gamers upload their own picture, a stock image anyone else can also buy is a weak thing to charge for. A frame composes with the photo they chose.
  • Anything XP- or level-gated. There is no such system, which is why the pass was dropped. Don't reintroduce one just to justify a cosmetic.
  • Cosmetics that affect matching or visibility. That is the consumables shelf's job, and keeping the two apart is what lets us say cosmetics are decoration only.

Rules anything new has to respect

Each of these has a test or a gate behind it — they are constraints, not preferences.

  1. Exactly one free item. The QA suite asserts the free catalogue is exactly Steel, and that something costs exactly 100. New categories launch paid-only.
  2. Free does not mean owned. Ownership is a row in gamer_cosmetic and nothing else; a free item is claimed at a price of zero.
  3. Coin spends go through the optimistic lock. A sink that skips the read-check-write under @Version will charge once and grant twice on a double tap.
  4. Membership items are withdrawn on lapse. The hourly job strips anything worn but not owned; anything granted with Gold inherits that.
  5. The art is generated and licence-free. Drawn from primitives — no sourced packs, no fonts, no logos, characters or sprites. Banners may evoke a genre; they may not borrow from a game. That is a legal rule before it is a taste one, because these are sold.
  6. Frames have a geometry contract. The hole radius in the generator and HOLE_RATIO in FramedAvatar are the same number twice.
  7. Banners are cropped and partly covered. The art is 1200×400 but renders nearer 4:1 with contentFit="cover", so the top and bottom sixth never appear — and the avatar sits over the bottom-left corner.

Suggested order

Drops, then animated banners, then username colour, then bundles. Limited-time drops first because they make everything already on the shelf worth more; animated banners next because they are pure generator work; username colour because it is the first genuinely new kind; bundles last of the four, because they need several items to bundle.

Leave the match-moment effect, profile themes and lobby accents until there is data on what actually sells. There is none today — the shop has never been open with a full catalogue — so ranking further than this would be a guess dressed up as a plan.

Instrument first. The one thing worth adding alongside the next drop is a count of purchases per cosmetic. Every argument above gets much shorter once we know whether the 1,500-coin animated frames sell at all.

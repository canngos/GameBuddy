# GameBuddy — Product Brief for Advertising

**Prepared for:** advertising partner producing a promotional video
**Date:** 21 August 2026
**App name (as it appears in the store):** GameBuddy: Find Gamers to Play
**Website:** findgamebuddy.com
**Platform:** Android first, iOS to follow
**Price:** Free to download and use, with an optional paid membership

---

## 1. In one sentence

**GameBuddy helps gamers find other people to play with — matched on the games they
actually own, the platform they play on, and the way they like to play.**

---

## 2. The problem it solves

Most people who play games online play alone, or with strangers they get randomly
dropped next to. Finding someone who owns the same game, plays it on the same console
or PC, is online at the same hours, and has the same attitude to it — that is
surprisingly hard.

The existing options are each bad in a specific way:

- **Random matchmaking inside the game** puts you with strangers you will never see again.
- **Big chat servers** are full of thousands of people, and finding the right two of them
  is its own job.
- **Forums and "looking for group" posts** are a wall of text that goes stale in an hour.

GameBuddy replaces all of that with something everyone already knows how to use: a
simple deck of profiles you swipe through.

---

## 3. Who it is for

Adults (18+) who play video games and want people to play *with* — not spectators, not
followers, not dates.

- The player whose friends have drifted away from the game they still love
- Someone who just bought a co-op game and has nobody to play it with
- A player who wants a regular squad instead of a different random team every night
- Someone who moved country and lost their gaming group

It is deliberately **not a dating app**, and that distinction matters a great deal to us —
see section 8.

---

## 4. How it works — the user journey

This is the sequence a video would naturally follow.

**Step 1 — Say what you play.**
New users pick their games from a catalogue of around 180 titles (Counter-Strike 2,
Elden Ring, Baldur's Gate III, League of Legends, Minecraft, Fortnite, Final Fantasy XIV
and so on), choose their platforms (PC, PlayStation, Xbox, Switch, mobile), and pick a
few tags describing how they play — around 80 of them, things like *tryhard*,
*completionist*, *chill co-op*, *voice chat*, *toxic-free*, *late-night*. It takes about
a minute.

**Step 2 — Swipe the deck.**
The app shows real gamer profiles one card at a time. Swipe **right** if you would want
to play together, **left** if not, **up** to send a *Super Like* — which tells that
person straight away. The order of the deck is not random: profiles are ranked by how
well they actually fit what you said you play.

**Step 3 — Match.**
When two people both swipe right, it is a match. That is the moment the app is built
around.

**Step 4 — Chat.**
A match opens a private conversation. Nobody can message you unless you both agreed to
it first.

**Step 5 — Go play.**
That is the whole point. The app deliberately gets out of the way once you have found
each other.

## 5. The main features

### The swipe deck
The core of the app. Profiles ranked by shared games, shared platforms and shared play
style — not by who happens to be geographically nearby.

### Lobbies
Instead of matching one-to-one, a player can post an **open lobby**: "Ranked, tonight at
9, mic required, 4 players." Other people browse the lobbies, ask to join, and whoever
opened it picks the team. There is a group chat for the squad to sort out the details.
Browsing and joining lobbies is free; opening your own is part of the paid membership.

### Chat
Private one-to-one chat with your matches, plus group chat inside lobbies. Live typing
indicators, read receipts and online status. Messages are stored encrypted.

### Friends
Beyond matches, people can add each other as friends.

### Profile, badges and cosmetics
Every player has a profile showing their games, platforms, play style, friends and
badges. Badges are earned by using the app — first match, first message, five friends,
ten matches — and there is a **Market** where players spend coins on decorative profile
frames and banners. Coins are earned free through a daily streak, weekly missions and
short optional videos, or bought.

### Notifications
Push notifications when you match, when someone messages you, and when someone wants
into your lobby.

### Seven languages
English, Finnish, Swedish, German, French, Spanish and Turkish — the whole app, not just
the store page.

### Light and dark themes
Both are fully designed. Dark is what most gamers will see.

## 6. Free vs. paid

**Everything essential is free.** Matching, chatting, lobbies, badges and profiles cost
nothing. Free users get 50 swipes a day, of which 15 can be likes.

**GameBuddy Gold** is the optional membership:

| Benefit | What it means |
|---|---|
| See who liked you | Every face, not just a count |
| No daily limit | Swipe and like as much as you want |
| Advanced filters | Narrow the deck by game, region, and who is online right now |
| Open your own lobbies | Free users can still browse and join any lobby |
| Gold frame and banner | A visible profile mark |

Pricing: **$3.99/week, $7.99/month (3-day free trial), or $39.99/year.** Coin packs are
sold separately from $1.99.

The message we want in any advertising: **Gold adds convenience; it does not lock away
the thing you came for.**

## 7. Safety — worth mentioning, briefly

This matters to users and to the app stores, and it is a genuine differentiator:

- **18+ only.** Age is calculated from a date of birth given at registration.
- **Every uploaded photo is screened automatically** before anybody else can see it.
- **Block and report anyone** from their profile or from a conversation. Reports are
  reviewed within 24 hours by a real moderation team.
- **Messages are encrypted** where they are stored. Moderators only see a message if it
  is reported.
- **Phone numbers and contact details are stripped** out of public text automatically.
- **Delete your account** in one tap from Settings, or from the website without
  installing anything.

A short line in a video — *"free, moderated, and 18+"* — carries all of this.

## 8. Positioning: what GameBuddy is NOT

Please avoid these framings entirely:

- **Not a dating app.** The swipe mechanic is borrowed; the purpose is not. No romantic
  or flirty framing, no "find your player two", no couples. The app has no "interested
  in" field and no gender filter — it cannot be used to look for a date. It is
  categorised on Google Play as an **App**, not under Dating, and advertising that
  implies otherwise creates a real store-policy problem for us.
- **Not a game.** GameBuddy contains no gameplay; it connects people who then go play
  something else.
- **Not a chat server or social network.** No feed, no followers, no posts.
- **Not for kids.** Adults only, and it should never look like it is aimed at teenagers.

## 9. Tone and personality

The product's own voice is **plain, dry and slightly understated** — it tells you what to
do and does not oversell. Real lines from the app and site:

> "Find people who actually play what you play."
> "People who play what you play, at the hours you actually play."
> "Ranked by what you play, not who is nearby."
> "Both of you have to say yes first."
> "Stop playing alone."
> "Then go play something."

It is gaming-native without being loud about it — no screaming esports energy, no
neon-overload clichés, no fake "epic" gameplay footage. Think *quietly confident*: a tool
that works, aimed at people for whom playing games is a normal part of the week.

The emotional beat we care most about: **the loneliness of playing alone, resolved.**
That is the story. Someone logs on, nobody is around, they queue solo again — and then
they do not have to.

## 12. Practical notes for the video

- **Show the swipe.** It is instantly understood and it is the core of the product.
- **Show a real match moment.** Two right swipes, then the chat opening.
- **Show the lobby.** It is the feature that most clearly separates us from a dating app —
  four people planning a session together.
- **Use dark mode** for most of it. It is what the audience expects and what looks best.
- **Real game titles matter.** Players recognise them instantly, but please clear any
  actual game names, logos or footage before use — we do not hold those rights.
- **Do not fake gameplay.** GameBuddy is what happens *before* you play. The payoff shot
  can be people playing, but the product being sold is the finding.
- **Adults, not "adult".** Keep it 18+ appropriate without being suggestive.

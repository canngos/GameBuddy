# GameBuddy — Child Safety Standards

**Version 2026-08-20.** These are GameBuddy's published standards against child sexual abuse
and exploitation (CSAE). They are referenced from the app's store listings and are the document
Google Play's Child Safety Standards policy requires a social app to publish.

GameBuddy is an adults-only service. Nobody under 18 may hold an account. That does not make
this document unnecessary — a service that shows photographs of strangers and opens private
conversations is exactly the kind of place people try to use for these purposes, whatever the
stated age of everybody on it — so what follows is written as an operating procedure rather
than as a statement of intent.

---

## 1. What these standards cover

**CSAE** — child sexual abuse and exploitation — means any conduct that sexualises, abuses or
exploits a person under 18. It includes, and is not limited to:

- **CSAM**, child sexual abuse material: any image or video depicting a minor in a sexual
  manner, including drawn, rendered or AI-generated depictions.
- **Grooming** — building a relationship with a minor in order to sexually abuse or exploit
  them, on this service or anywhere else.
- **Sextortion** — coercing anybody with sexual imagery, and coercing a minor by any means.
- **Trafficking**, or advertising a minor for sexual purposes.
- **Solicitation and sexual contact** — sending sexual content to a minor, asking a minor for
  sexual imagery of themselves, sexual conversation or roleplay with a minor, or arranging to
  meet a minor for sexual purposes.
- **Sexualised commentary** about a minor, and the sharing of links, usernames, invitations or
  codes that lead to any of the above on another service.

A minor is anybody under 18, regardless of what any account claims.

## 2. Our position

**There is no tolerance for any of this on GameBuddy, and none of it requires a pattern of
behaviour before we act.** A single instance is enough to terminate an account permanently.

The prohibition is stated in our [Terms of Service](https://findgamebuddy.com/terms), which
every account holder accepts at registration, and it is written there in the same terms it is
written here: content that sexualises any person under 18 in any way is forbidden, and using
GameBuddy to move somebody to another service in order to do something forbidden here is
forbidden too.

## 3. Nobody under 18 holds an account

- Registration asks for a **date of birth**, not an age. The date is validated on the server,
  not by the app, and an account it puts under 18 is refused outright.
- There is **no younger tier, no parental-consent route and no supervised mode**. There is no
  configuration of GameBuddy in which a minor is a legitimate user.
- If we learn that an account holder is under 18, **we close the account and delete the data
  associated with it**, without waiting for the account holder to respond.
- Anyone — account holder or not — can tell us that an account belongs to a minor by writing
  to **contact@findgamebuddy.com**. We act on those reports whether or not the person
  reporting can prove it; the cost of closing an adult's account in error is far lower than
  the alternative.

**What this is, and what it is not.** A date of birth checked on the server is stronger than a
number typed into a settings field, and it does not go stale. It is still a self-declaration,
and we say so plainly rather than describe it as verification. It is the reason the rest of
this document exists.

## 4. Screening before anything is published

- **Every uploaded profile photograph is screened by an automated classifier before any other
  account can see it.** Images the classifier is confident are sexual are refused. Images it is
  unsure about are **held unpublished, visible only to their owner, until a human decides** —
  an uncertain verdict is never treated as approval.
- **Text is screened on every surface where it is typed** — profiles, direct messages and
  lobbies. Slurs and sexual abuse are refused rather than masked, and on public surfaces email
  addresses, links and phone numbers are stripped, because moving a conversation off-platform
  is the first move in most of what section 1 describes.
- **A private conversation requires a mutual match.** No account can open a conversation with
  another that has not independently chosen them, which removes unsolicited contact as a route.
- **Blocking is immediate, mutual and silent.** A blocked account cannot see, match with, or
  continue a conversation with the person who blocked it, and is not told that it happened.

**The limits, stated honestly.** The image classifier answers one narrow question — does this
look sexual — probabilistically. It does not recognise a minor, a photograph taken without
consent, or a picture of a screen showing either. A word filter does not understand context.
Automated screening raises the cost of casual abuse and buys time; it is not a moderation
policy. What protects people is the report control and a human who answers it.

## 5. Reporting, inside the app and outside it

**In the app, without leaving it.** Every profile carries a report control, reachable from
anywhere that profile appears. Every message in a conversation can be reported by the person
who received it. Both sit beside a block control, so somebody can stop the contact first and
explain afterwards. Reports are recorded once per reporter per item, so a report count means
what it appears to mean.

**Outside the app.** Anybody — including someone who does not use GameBuddy, and including a
parent, a guardian, a hotline or a police force — can report to **contact@findgamebuddy.com**.
No account is needed.

**What we commit to.** Every report is reviewed and acted on **within 24 hours**. Acting means
removing the content, terminating the account, or recording that the report was not founded.

**A report alleging CSAE is not held to that window.** It is triaged ahead of everything else
in the queue and acted on as soon as it is seen, because the 24 hours exist to bound how long
an ordinary complaint waits, not to license a delay on this one. What acting means here is
section 6.

## 6. What happens to CSAE and CSAM

When a report, or our own review, gives us actual knowledge of CSAE:

1. **The content is removed immediately** and stops being served to anybody. We do not wait for
   confirmation from anyone else before removing it; a wrongly removed photograph can be
   restored, and the alternative cannot be undone.
2. **The account is terminated permanently.** Not suspended, not warned. Any other account we
   can attribute to the same person goes with it.
3. **The material and the account records are preserved rather than deleted**, out of reach of
   ordinary deletion, so that they remain available to law enforcement. This is a deliberate
   exception to our usual practice of deleting data when an account closes: the obligation to
   preserve evidence overrides the ordinary erasure route, and our
   [Privacy Policy](https://findgamebuddy.com/privacy) is where what we keep, and for how long,
   is set out.
4. **We report it, in every case.** Confirmed CSAM is reported to the **National Center for
   Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC)** through the CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org,
   which accepts reports from providers outside the United States, and to the authorities in
   our own jurisdiction: the **Finnish Police** (emergency 112; poliisi.fi) and **Nettivihje**,
   the Finnish reporting hotline operated by Save the Children Finland and a member of the
   INHOPE network. Reporting is not conditional on where the account holder is, on whether the
   material was shared with anybody, or on our being able to identify anyone involved.
5. **We cooperate.** We respond to lawful requests from police forces, courts and recognised
   hotlines, and we do not require a report to have arrived through the app before we act on it.
6. **We correct our own mistakes.** An account holder who believes their account was terminated
   in error can write to the address in section 7 and a person will look at it again. This is a
   route for a wrong decision, not an appeal against a confirmed one: where the material is what
   it appeared to be, the termination stands and is not negotiable.

We do not conduct our own investigation beyond establishing that the content is what it appears
to be. Identifying and pursuing an offender is a matter for the authorities, and an amateur
investigation risks the evidence they need.

## 8. Keeping this document true

One person operates GameBuddy, reviews the moderation queue, and answers the address above. We
would rather say that than imply a trust and safety department that does not exist — what
matters to somebody making a report is whether the commitment in section 5 is met, and it is a
commitment made in full knowledge of who has to meet it.

These standards are reviewed **at least once a year**, and whenever the service changes in a way
that affects any of them. The version string at the top of this document changes when the text
does.

our word for it.

Where the coins go

An engaged free player was earning about 950 coins a week against a documented target of 250–400, and rewarded adverts were paying out four to eight times what they earned. Here is the audit, what changed, and what is still open.

31 August 2026 Companion to monetization-analysis.md Changes are shipped, not proposed

The stated target

Coins are what the shop is priced in, so how easily they arrive decides whether anybody ever buys a pack. Three faults compounded, and all three were invisible: every rate was a hardcoded Java constant, and the admin dashboard reported only two aggregate sums, so nothing could say which faucet the coins had come from.

What was shipped, and the three faults

  1. The advert faucet shipped at more than twice its spec. 20 × 5 where the plan said 15 × 3 — +385 coins a week on its own, and enough to miss the target by itself.
  2. The streak stopped rewarding streaks. The ladder clamped at its last rung, so day 8, day 80 and day 800 all paid 25: an unconditional payment for opening the app.
  3. “Daily” did not mean daily. A 24-hour cooldown with a 48-hour grace let an unbroken run survive on about 3.5 claims a week — half the days, the full ladder.

What it cost in real money. A rewarded impression clears roughly $0.010–0.020, so five views a day earn $0.05–0.10 — and paid out 100 coins, which at the $1.99 / 500 pack is $0.40 of pack value.

The app was giving away four to eight times what the advert brought in, and a free user matched the smallest coin pack every five days. There is no version of that where somebody buys coins.

Where a full week of coins comes from, after:

Daily streak 140 Quests 75 Adverts 315

320 a week is the number to hold on to. It sits inside the band, and it is only reachable by turning up and watching an advert — passive income alone caps at 215. The full 530 needs every claim and all three adverts every day, which should feel like effort rather than the default. It is asserted in CoinFaucetTest, so it cannot drift again quietly.

And the rates are deployable now

They moved out of Java constants into CoinEconomyProperties, bound from gamebuddy.coins.* with COINS_* environment overrides. Retuning is a deploy, not a release, and the defaults are exactly what this build was tested with — an environment that sets nothing behaves as verified.

The client stopped keeping its own copies too: GET /coins/earn now returns the ladder and the advert amount, and the streak strip renders from those. That mattered — it was half the reason the numbers were never retuned, because changing them needed an app release to stop the UI lying about them.

And the ledger is finally readable: analytics now group coin movement by reason, so the advert share of issuance is a number on the dashboard rather than something you have to go looking for. In a month it should have fallen from roughly three quarters toward a half.

Still open

  • Multi-accounting. The advert cap is per account and nothing bounds the number of accounts. The verification itself is sound — real server-side verification, and the transaction id is claimed before any coins move, so a replayed callback pays nothing.
  • No rate limit on the earn endpoints. /coins/earn/** and /ads/reward sit outside the rate-limit namespace. Not exploitable today — every claim is idempotent against its own state — but recommend 30/min and 30/day per account.
  • The advert user id is not bound to the session. Signed by Google in transit, so it cannot be tampered with, but nothing ties it to who was logged in. A gifting vector rather than a minting one, and it consumes the victim’s cap.
  • Doc drift. monetization-analysis.md §1.3 still says 975 coins from 13 badges and a 9,400-coin catalogue; it is 775 from 10 and 18,450 across 34 items.
  • The sinks are still finite. Retuning income buys time; it does not give a long-term player somewhere to spend. That is what limited-time drops are for.
